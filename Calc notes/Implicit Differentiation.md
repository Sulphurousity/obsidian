**Implicit** - When you cannot get y alone, for relations not just functions
Any relation that can be solved for y is explicit while any relation that cannot be solved for y is implicit

Ex: $y=x^2+2x$ is explicit
$$\large {1}\frac{dy}{dx}=2x{\frac{dy}{dx}}+2{\frac{dy}{dx}}$$

Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$:
$$\large x^2+y^2=1, y\geq_{0}$$
$$\large y^2=1-x$$
$$\large y=\sqrt{ (1-x^2) }=(1-x^2)^\frac{1}{2}$$
$$\large y'=\frac{1}{2}(1-x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-2x)=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 -x^2 }}$$

Implicit Diff:
$$\large x^2+y^2=1$$
$$\large 2x{\frac{dy}{dx}} + 2y{\frac{dy}{dx}} = 0$$
$$\large y{\frac{dy}{dx}}=-x$$
$$\large \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{-x}{y}$$

Method:
1 - Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$
2 - Solve for $\frac{dy}{dx}$
